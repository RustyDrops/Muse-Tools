# Cinematic Video — Gate Before Generation

Tell Muse "how to shoot" rather than "make it look like a movie."

## Hard gate — no exceptions

Do NOT generate any video until the user has supplied / approved ALL of the intake below, you have walked them through it, and they have given an explicit go-ahead for that exact version.

If a request says just "make a video of X" — STOP. Walk through the intake, propose drafts for the missing pieces, present the shot plan for approval. Only then generate.

## Intake — the six categories (all required)

Organize every brief into exactly these six:

1. **Duration and framing** — total duration, number of shots/clips, aspect ratio, framing per shot (wide / medium / close-up / behind), subject size in frame.
2. **People** — who, how many, appearance / reference image, expression and emotion to land, gaze direction, wardrobe if it matters.
3. **Location** — exact place, time of day, season, key colours and landmarks.
4. **Movement by the second** — second-by-second action timeline per shot; what moves, what stays still, how movement continues between shots.
5. **Camera work** — per shot: static / pan / push-in / tracking, speed, angle, height; lighting direction and softness, especially on faces.
6. **Sound and things to avoid** — music / ambience / dialogue / silence and mood; explicit avoid list.

Missing any category = do not generate. Propose a draft for the user to approve / correct instead.

## Assemble a small film crew before generating

Present the plan in these roles before any generation:

- **Director** — what is this scene for, what should the viewer feel / remember?
- **Screenplay** — story beats in order, one purpose per shot
- **Cinematography** — framing, camera work, light, subject priority
- **Art direction** — location, colours, background vs foreground, props
- **Sound** — audio plan and avoid list

## Shot structure — don't cram everything into one shot

- Give each shot ONE purpose.
- Default flow when it fits: wide shot to establish location → close-up for emotion → shot from behind to leave a lingering impression.
- If a single-clip tool forces one shot, state that limit, pick the single purpose that matters most, and get the user to confirm that trade-off before generating.

## Subject priority

- When the user says the face / person is the priority, that outranks scenery — no matter how beautiful the background is. Subject large enough that eyes and expression read clearly, soft light on the face.
- Keep prized background elements IN THE BACKGROUND. Keep the space in front of the face / lens CLEAR unless explicitly asked otherwise — foreground elements that hide the face or distract the eye go on the avoid list by default.

## Continuity for follow-ups

- Carry over the final frame of the previous video as the starting image / reference for the next one.
- Match the person's position and gaze, the direction of light, and the continuation of movement — not just the image, the movement too.
- Confirm the final frame is in hand before generating. If you don't have it, get it first — do not invent continuity.

## Walkthrough before generation

1. Restate the goal in one line (Director view).
2. Present the six-category brief as filled so far, marking anything missing / assumed.
3. Present the shot list — shot #, purpose, framing, camera, duration.
4. Present crew notes: story, composition/colour, sound, avoid list.
5. Call out the #1 priority for this video and how the plan protects it.
6. For a follow-up: confirm final-frame carry-over.
7. Ask for explicit approval of that exact plan. Only after approval, generate.
8. After generation, view the result yourself before calling it ready — check face visibility, foreground clearance, continuity.

## Example

Nanzen-ji Temple, Kyoto. Red autumn leaves, yellow ginkgo, blue sky.
Priority: the woman's face / expression over the scenery. Soft light on her face, eyes visible.
Leaves in background only, clear space in front of her face — no swirling leaves in front of the lens.
Goal: not just a beautiful landscape, but a scene that stays in memory.
