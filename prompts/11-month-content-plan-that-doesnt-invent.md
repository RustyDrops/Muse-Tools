# 11 — A month of social posts that doesn't invent anything

Adapted from a viral 5-prompt "run my Instagram month" thread (@Jeffar_AI, X,
2026-11-18), tested end-to-end and fixed. The original steps are good; the
original prompts will happily invent your results, your stories, and a posting
load you can't sustain. This version keeps the steps and adds the guards.

## The setup (do this once)
Tell your Muse: your business, your audience, your goal, 3-4 content pillars,
how many posts a week you can actually do, how many minutes a week you have,
and your town. If you have three best-performing posts, name them. If you
don't — you're new, or dormant — say "no data yet" out loud. That one sentence
changes everything below.

## The 5 steps
1. **Month system.** "Build a month of content: weekly themes, the video /
   carousel / graphic mix, a hook for every post, a caption structure, and
   the calendar in order — using only my cadence and minutes. Any date or
   deadline must be marked VERIFIED against the official source, or left as
   [verify before drafting]. If I said no data yet, build from my goal and
   audience; do not invent past results."
2. **Hook bank.** "Write 15 hooks for [topic], under 8 words each: some
   controversial, surprising, problem, result, and question hooks. Rank them
   and draft the first two caption lines for the top five. Tag every hook
   PLANNED or PROVEN — a result hook is PROVEN only if I gave you the
   evidence. A PLANNED result is a filming goal, never a published claim."
3. **Carousel builder.** "Turn [topic] into a carousel: title slide, one idea
   per slide, one example per slide, a call to action on the last slide,
   every slide readable in under five seconds. Use the fewest slides that
   carry it — 10 is the ceiling, not the target. Examples are patterns or
   stories I supplied, never invented people."
4. **Captions + hashtags.** "For each post: a strong first line, one clear
   value, a story or example, and a call to action, plus exactly 7 hashtags —
   3 broad, 2 niche, 2 for my town or audience. The story must be labelled:
   a general pattern, one I supplied, or none. No invented anecdotes."
5. **Stories, scaled.** "Plan one week of stories with polls, questions, and
   behind-the-scenes — exact text, which sticker, best time. Scale it to my
   minutes: about 2 hours a week means one story per posting day, not three
   a day. If my audience is children, behind-the-scenes means objects and
   workspaces, never a required photo of a child."

## The rules that make it trustworthy
- Drafts only. You approve the exact version before anything posts.
- A deadline you haven't verified doesn't ship.
- End the month by feeding the real numbers back in as next month's
  "three best posts" — that's when step 1 starts earning its keep.
